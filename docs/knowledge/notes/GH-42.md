# GH-42: Redesign Presenter as a ViewModel (remove View reference)

## Notes

### 2026-09-15: Research-WU — coroutines adoption scoping

Topic-mode research, one subagent, four sub-question search passes. Full evidence tables in the
subagent's report; synthesis below.

**1. `kotlinx-coroutines-core` version vs. Kotlin 1.9.24.** No single sentence found stating
"1.8.1 targets Kotlin 1.9.x" outright, but the changelog structure supports it: 1.8.0's entry
says "Major Kotlin version update: was 1.8.20, became 1.9.21"; 1.9.0's entry says "Kotlin was
updated to 2.0". So 1.8.x is the last minor line built against Kotlin 1.9.x, inferred rather than
stated. **Action for WU1:** treat 1.8.1 as the working version, confirmed empirically by
`./gradlew build` succeeding — the build itself is the authoritative check here, not the
changelog.

**2. `runTest` + hand-written fakes.** No documented general incompatibility. The real, officially
documented hazard is narrower and directly relevant to this plan's design: a coroutine that
`launch`es and runs for the test's duration (e.g. **the View's request-handler coroutine draining
`Presenter.uiRequests`**, exactly what `FakePresenterUi.drive`/`ViewImpl.play`/`TuiView.play`'s
handler does) makes plain `runTest` hang waiting for it to finish, unless it's launched in
`TestScope.backgroundScope` (auto-cancelled at test end) — official KDoc
(kotlinx.coroutines-test `backgroundScope`) plus a corroborating community write-up. Since
nothing in this codebase uses real `delay()`, `runTest`'s virtual-time skip buys nothing here;
plain `runBlocking` is an equally valid, simpler alternative per kt.academy and multiple
secondary sources. **Action for WU2/WU3:** launch each handler coroutine in `backgroundScope` in
any test using `runTest`, or use plain `runBlocking` throughout and skip the virtual-time
machinery entirely (still get `kotlinx-coroutines-test`'s `runTest` for the *timeout*-guard value
in `PresenterCancellationTest`/`PresenterOrderingTest` specifically, where nothing is launched in
the background).

**3. Rendezvous channel as a request/response seam.** The primitive itself is solidly documented
— the official Kotlin "Coroutines and channels" guide describes `Channel.RENDEZVOUS`'s
suspend-until-met semantics directly ("send()/receive() should 'meet on time'"). No named
precedent was found for the specific bidirectional "ask and suspend until answered" composition
(`UiRequest.respond` via `CompletableDeferred` paired with a rendezvous `send`) as an established
idiom in an official guide or well-known talk — closest found was generic one-directional
ViewModel→UI event-channel usage. Not a red flag (it's a straightforward composition of two
well-documented primitives, `Channel` + `CompletableDeferred`), but the plan's design here is
original composition, not a copied pattern — worth extra scrutiny in WU2's dedicated ordering/
deadlock tests rather than leaning on external precedent.

**4. `CancellationException`-before-`catch(Exception)` idiom.** Confirmed directly by an official,
quotable source: Android Developers' "Coroutines best practices" — "don't consume exceptions of
type CancellationException (don't catch them, or always rethrow them if caught). Prefer catching
specific exception types like IOException over generic types like Exception or Throwable." The
kotlinlang.org coroutines guide corroborates this implicitly (cancellation exceptions are
"transparent and unwrapped by default") but the exact canonical sentence from the
"Cancellation and exceptions" guide page couldn't be fetched (transient fetch failure, not
followed up further since the Android page already gives a solid official citation). **Confirms
the plan's hard-problem-3 fix as-designed** — no change needed.

**Net effect on the approved plan:** no changes to the design or the two approved decisions.
One concrete addition to WU2/WU3's test-writing: use `backgroundScope` (if using `runTest`) for
any test that launches a request-handler coroutine, to avoid the exact hang class this research
surfaced.

## Debrief summary

### 2026-09-15

**What went well**
- The four-WU split (suspend propagation → request channel → console-loop/interface collapse →
  mechanical rename) kept each WU's diff reviewable and each review/audit pass focused - no WU
  needed more than one fix round.
- Verifying a pure mechanical rename by checking diff symmetry (every `+` line maps to a token
  substitution of every `-` line, nothing else) caught the scope of WU4's change cheaply and
  gave the architectural review something concrete to state instead of a vague "looks fine."

**What was hard or surprising**
- Merged PR #47 (the final WU) immediately on user approval, then invoked `debrief` - the wrong
  order. `FEATURE.md` step 12 already says debrief runs before merge, but that's the opposite of
  what every prior WU in this ticket trained: WU1-3 merge immediately on approval with no
  debrief step at all. The final WU's approval order quietly inverts the habit built up over the
  previous three WUs, and nothing at the point of merging flagged the inversion.
- `audit` (THOROUGH/opus) was the single largest token consumer across this ticket's four WUs
  per the user's usage command, despite most of its findings across the ticket being small
  (a stale KDoc reference, a silent-no-op template substitution). The cost didn't track the
  size of the findings.

**What to change**
- `docs/workflows/MULTI_WU.md` now calls out the final-WU approval-order inversion explicitly,
  next to the debrief step, so the merge-immediately habit doesn't carry over by default.
- Downgraded both `audit` and `review` from their prior tiers to `FOCUSED`/`TACTICAL` in
  `docs/MODEL_TIERS.md` and their own `SKILL.md` frontmatter - `audit` was `THOROUGH`/`TACTICAL`
  (opus), `review` was `FOCUSED`/`ARCHITECTURAL` (already sonnet, now matches `audit`'s new
  tier exactly). Worth watching on the next few tickets: `audit` still needs to catch real
  cross-file issues (this ticket's `setup.sh` template-breakage finding was genuine and worth
  the pass), so if finding quality visibly drops at the lower tier, revert.
