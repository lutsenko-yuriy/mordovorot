# GH-213: Fast solver for Mordie (3-cycle solver in shared/mordie)

## Notes

### 2026-11-01

**Kotlin measurements (a throwaway test, not committed)**

100 seeded random solvable boards per size, steps of one tile (`step(1)`), `TimeSource.Monotonic`, after two warm-up rounds. Lengths are in our moves; every solution is replayed to solved by `ThreeCycleSolverTest`. Times in microseconds.

| Size | Moves, mean / max | JVM: solve mean / max, worst step | Node.js: solve mean / max, worst step |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3x3 | 24.4 / 32 | 0 / 3, 1 | 4 / 18, 13 |
| 4x4 | 53.8 / 64 | 3 / 39, 12 | 10 / 74, 4 |
| 5x5 | 90.3 / 104 | 3 / 8, 4 | 30 / 56, 14 |
| 6x6 | 134.5 / 153 | 6 / 10, 4 | 45 / 289, 28 |
| 7x7 | 187.5 / 205 | 8 / 22, 6 | 69 / 171, 71 |
| 8x8 | 251.6 / 269 | 12 / 51, 18 | 82 / 141, 34 |
| 9x9 | 321.5 / 339 | 19 / 38, 15 | 117 / 446, 308 |

- A whole 9x9 solve takes under half a millisecond and the worst single step 0.3 ms, so one step is far inside a 10 ms slice (the ticket's second open question).
- The last row's setup is a direct construction (column down by 1, then row 0, undo after the 3-cycle), 8 moves per tile; no search (the first open question).
- Lengths match the Python prototype of GH-116 within a few percent (4x4: 53.8 against 49.3, whose setup search found shorter last-row cycles).
