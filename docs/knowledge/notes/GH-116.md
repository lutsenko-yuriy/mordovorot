**Survey of fast torus-puzzle solvers (topic mode, one subagent)**

The subagent searched four sub-questions: constructive algorithms, automated human methods, scalable heuristic search, and measured results on large boards. I allowed up to 5 sources per sub-question (11 rows in total) instead of the skill's 3 to 5, because one table could not cover four questions. Metric: STM counts every single-step shift; MTM counts a run on one line as one move, and Amano et al.'s "drag" is the same idea. MTM <= STM.

**Synthesis**

- Amano et al. is the only source with a constructive bound in a metric close to ours: about 4mn moves, linear in the tile count (roughly 36 plus a constant on 3x3, 324 plus 9c on 9x9). It is a worst-case bound, not a measurement.
- The torchlight gist is the only code that handles any size and parity, so it is a ready prototype; its bound is in STM and its real lengths in our moves are unknown.
- Table-based IDA* exists for 4x4 and 5x5 only. The fast 5x5 variant needs a 128 MB table, which doesn't fit the web bundle; the smaller variant's table size isn't stated.
- Nothing found for: weighted or relaxed IDA*, beam search or similar on this puzzle; automated human methods (fringe-first, commutators) with reported lengths; time or length on 6x6 to 9x9.
- The 6x6 STM upper bound appears as 86 (an earlier chat), 88 (coolcomputery) and "114 111" (a forum snippet); nothing resolves it.

**Shortfall**

The SpeedSolving thread, the source the ticket relies on for asymptotics and the MTM/STM bounds, returned 403 on every route, so its numbers (rows 4 and 11) are snippet-level, as are the God's numbers in the ticket's source chats. The "Rubik's on the torus" paper (Alm et al., 2013) was not searched; the ticket does not need it. Rows 3 and 8 to 10 are partly verified. More than half of the table is not snippet-only. No source has measurements for 6x6 to 9x9, so the measurement step is the core of the research, not a check on it.

**Prototypes to measure first**

1. Amano's construction.
2. The torchlight Shell-sort gist.
3. A fringe-first method with conjugated 3-cycles.
4. The 5x5 two-phase IDA* only as a quality reference.

**Measurement 1: torchlight Shell-sort gist (Python, unmodified)**

Random boards (a seeded shuffle, with the GH-74 fix-up on odd sizes), 100 per size up to 6x6 and 30 per size from 7x7. Every solution was replayed and ended solved (590 of 590). Moves counted our way: consecutive moves on one line merged, a run that nets to zero dropped. Times are Python on this machine, so only the order of magnitude carries over to Kotlin.

| Size | Time, mean / max (ms) | Our moves, mean / max | Single shifts, mean / max |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3x3 | 0.1 / 0.1 | 46 / 83 | 50 / 92 |
| 4x4 | 0.2 / 0.6 | 143 / 204 | 193 / 264 |
| 5x5 | 0.5 / 0.7 | 306 / 399 | 441 / 573 |
| 6x6 | 0.9 / 1.1 | 510 / 632 | 859 / 1046 |
| 7x7 | 1.3 / 1.6 | 828 / 1034 | 1454 / 1783 |
| 8x8 | 1.9 / 2.3 | 1134 / 1254 | 2193 / 2358 |
| 9x9 | 2.5 / 3.1 | 1453 / 1655 | 2898 / 3271 |

The solver is fast enough that a slice is not a concern: a whole 9x9 solve took 3 ms even in Python. Its solutions are long, about 10 times the optimum where that is known (4x4: about 13.8 single shifts for the optimal IDA* in torchlight/loopsolver, against 193 here). It does cover every size, including the ones Mordie sits out. Moves per tile (mean) rise from 5 on 3x3 to 9, 12, 14, 17 and 18, and stay at 18 on 9x9, so length grows a little faster than the tile count over this range; seven sizes are too few to fit a curve.

**Measurement 2: Amano-style 3-cycle solver, and the true minimum on 3x3 and 4x4**

I implemented Amano et al.'s idea myself (throwaway Python, in the scratchpad, nothing in the repo): place tile 0, 1, 2, ... in order, each with one 3-cycle of the tile's cell, the target and a third cell from the unsolved area. A 3-cycle is a commutator of a row move and a column move (4 moves) when the three cells form an L; otherwise it is conjugated by 1 or 2 setup moves (6 or 8 moves). On boards with an odd arrangement and an even side, one shift up front fixes the parity. The last two tiles fall into place by themselves. The paper's seven-move three-element rotation for the last row is replaced by this setup search, so this is the paper's idea, not its exact construction.

Same boards for both solvers (100 on 3x3, 60 on 4x4, 100 on 5x5 and 6x6, 40 on 7x7, 30 on 8x8 and 9x9; seeded); every solution replayed to a solved board. Our moves, mean per board. "Reordered" merges moves of one line that commute (row moves commute with row moves, columns with columns), a free post-pass.

| Size | Shell-sort gist | gist, reordered | 3-cycles | 3-cycles, max | 3-cycles, ms (Python) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3x3 | 44.4 | 41.7 | 24.3 | 32 | 0.2 |
| 4x4 | 145.2 | 138.3 | 49.3 | 63 | 1.2 |
| 5x5 | 301.4 | 289.8 | 87.4 | 103 | 5.6 |
| 6x6 | 509.1 | 488.4 | 133.1 | 149 | 16.1 |
| 7x7 | 823.0 | 792.4 | 183.0 | 203 | 41.6 |
| 8x8 | 1144.5 | 1102.3 | 246.4 | 262 | 87.8 |
| 9x9 | 1457.0 | 1410.5 | 316.6 | 339 | 173.3 |

- **Against the minimum.** Mordie's exact search (the real `IdaStarSolver`, same boards) gives a mean of 6.23 moves on 3x3 (max 8) and 11.53 on 4x4 (max 13). The 3-cycle solver is 4.0x the minimum on 3x3 and 4.3x on 4x4; the gist is 7.3x and 12.6x.
- **Exact search time** on the JVM: 3x3 about 0 ms; 4x4 mean 4.5 s, median 2.4 s, max 36 s. The fast solver answers in about 1 ms there.
- **Length per tile** for the 3-cycle solver: 3.9 moves per tile on 9x9, matching Amano's 4mn bound (324 for 81 tiles), well under his worst case. Shell sort needs 18.
- **Reordering** saves about 3 to 5% for the gist and nothing for the 3-cycle solver (its moves already alternate row and column).
- **Slice fit.** One tile placement takes at most 3 ms on 5x5 and 39 ms on 9x9 in Python. The slow part is the brute-force search for a setup in the last row, so a Kotlin version would need a direct formula for the setup (or a time check in the loop) to stay within a 10 ms slice. A placement is an independent step, so the solver is naturally resumable: its whole state is the board and the next tile.

**Not run**

- A fringe-first method (first row and column, then the rest) was not built. After the frame is solved the rest is no longer a torus, so the same L-shape trick doesn't apply directly, and the row-by-row 3-cycle solver above covers the hand-solving idea.
- The 5x5 two-phase IDA* (torchlight/loopsolver, Java) was not run. Its README gives 31.5 single shifts on average in 0.4 to 0.8 s with tables up to 128 MB, so it stays a reference, not a candidate.
- Kotlin timings: all times above are Python on this machine.

**Evaluation against the constraints (RESEARCH.md step 3)**

Constraints that decide it: shared Kotlin code for the terminal and the browser, no backend (anything precomputed must ship in the bundle), the browser's single thread (slices of about 10 ms, until GH-211 decides where the search runs), a solo developer, and a hobby prototype.

| Option | Fit | What could go wrong |
|---|---|---|
| A. 3-cycle solver (Amano's idea) | Best. Plain code, no tables, every size 3x3 to 9x9, resumable one tile at a time, 4 to 4.3x the minimum where known, 317 moves on 9x9 | The last-row setup search took up to 39 ms per tile in Python, so it needs a formula or a time check to fit a slice. Quality beyond 4x4 is unknown (no minimum to compare with). Solutions of hundreds of moves make the step list long (a later ticket) |
| B. Shell-sort solver (torchlight gist, the O(N log² N) idea) | Fits the code constraints (small, no tables) | longer than A on every size (1.8x on 3x3, 2.9x on 4x4, 4.6x on 9x9), and its edge is asymptotic (huge boards), beyond the 9x9 we offer. No reason to build it unless A can't be made to fit a slice |
| C. Table-based IDA* (torchlight/loopsolver) | Poor. Shortest of the fast options on 5x5 (31.5 single shifts), but tables of up to 128 MB, only 4x4 and 5x5 | Doesn't fit a web bundle; no help for 6x6 and up, which are the sizes Mordie sits out |
| D. Keep the exact search only | Zero cost | 5x5 never gets an answer and 6x6 to 9x9 stay sat out, which is the problem the ticket exists for |
| E. Shorten a fast solution afterwards | Untested beyond the free reordering (3 to 5% on the gist, none on A) | A real improvement pass (e.g. replacing short windows of moves by shorter ones) would need its own experiment |

**Recommendation (draft, for the user's approval)**

1. **Build option A first**, in the shared Mordie module, as the fast solver running alongside the exact search. It is the only candidate that covers every size, needs no tables, and is already within 4.3x of the minimum on the sizes where that can be checked.
2. **Don't build B or C.** B only matters beyond 9x9, and C's tables don't fit the bundle and stop at 5x5.
3. **Put a bound on one step**: place one tile per step, and replace the brute-force setup search for the last row with a direct construction (or a time check), so a step fits a 10 ms slice. Confirm with a Kotlin measurement before wiring it to the UI.
4. **Keep E as an experiment**, to run only if the lengths turn out to feel silly in play (hundreds of moves on 9x9). The exact search already replaces the fast answer on 3x3 and 4x4 within seconds.
5. **Suggested follow-up tickets, in order:**
   - The fast solver in `shared/mordie` (Kotlin, tests that replay solutions on all sizes and both parities), measured in Kotlin first.
   - Mordie using it alongside the exact search: the best-known solution, the "I found one in N moves, maybe there's shorter!" line, the announcement of a shorter one, Give Up available at the first solution, and 6x6 to 9x9 no longer sat out. This changes the spec, the saves and the lines, so it will probably split into several tickets.
   - Where it runs in the browser: GH-211.
   - Showing or saving a long solution (the walkthrough as a text or save file), already planned.
   - Shortening a fast solution (E), only if needed.
6. **Open points for those tickets:** quality on 5x5 and up is unmeasured (no minimum known; only the length is); the exact wording of the new lines; how saves keep the best solution so far.

No ADR: this is a recommendation for tickets, not yet a standing decision.
